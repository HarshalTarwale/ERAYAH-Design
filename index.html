What jadau actually is

Jadau is not a stone or a metal. It is a method: gold is warmed until it is soft enough to hold a stone without a claw, and the setter presses each polki, kundan or zircon into the metal itself. Nothing grips the stone except the gold around it.

It is slow, it is unforgiving, and it is the reason a jadau piece catches light the way it does. We have kept the method and changed everything around it — lighter frames, shorter drops, fewer stones, so a piece sits as easily with linen as it does with a lehenga.
