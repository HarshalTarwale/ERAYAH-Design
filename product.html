Each Meherbaan chandbali begins as a hand-drawn sheet of silver alloy. The frame is cut, filed and given its scalloped edge before a single stone goes in. The setter then warms the plated gold until it gives, and presses each glass polki into the metal itself — no claws, no glue. Twenty-one steps, roughly nine days per pair.

The drop is deliberately shorter than a traditional chandbali so it clears a collar, and the weight is kept under 14g a side so it can be worn through a full evening.

Gold plating wears with contact, not with time. Put your jewellery on last — after perfume, hairspray and moisturiser — and take it off first. Wipe with the dry cloth in your box after wearing, and store each piece in its own pouch so stones do not knock against each other.

Avoid water, steam and chlorine. If the plating dulls within six months of purchase, send it back and we will re-plate it at no cost.

Dispatched within 2 working days, insured, and delivered across India in 3–6 days at no charge. International shipping is calculated at checkout.

Why it feels lighter than it looks

A traditional chandbali of this size sits around 22g a side. Ours is 13.6g. We hollow the back plate, drop two rows of stones from the fan, and shorten the drop by 11mm — which is enough to take the pull off the earlobe without losing the silhouette.

It is the same setting method our karigars have used for three generations. Only the proportions have changed.
